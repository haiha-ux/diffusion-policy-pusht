Diffusion Policy on PushT (state-based)

What is live: the page runs two exported models in your browser through onnxruntime-web 1.22.0: the diffusion noise model (1D conditional U-Net, DDIM with 10 steps, sampled here in JavaScript) and the chunked BC network, both seed 0. Drag the T or the agent (blue circle), or turn the angle slider; the page then samples 12 action chunks from the diffusion model (coloured) and one from the BC network (black). Each line is the sequence of 16 absolute target positions the agent would be told to go to; the thick part is the first 8, the part a receding-horizon controller actually executes before re-planning.

What is not live: there is no physics in this page, so nothing here shows what the T does after the agent moves. The paths are open-loop predictions for the state you set (the agent is assumed to be at rest). The green T is the goal. The three GIFs at the bottom are pre-rendered closed-loop episodes from the Python simulator.

Spread between the coloured lines is the model's own uncertainty about where to go; if they overlap the model is nearly deterministic at that state. In the repository's measurement on held-out demo states the samples were close together in most states (see README, multimodality), so do not expect two clearly separate routes everywhere. Dragging into poses far from anything in the 206 demonstrations gives unreliable output.
